The case

Stakeholder: The Jordan Pharmacists Association working group on rural access, with the Jordan Food and Drug Administration observing

Several villages in Tafileh, Ma’an and the Jordan Valley have lost their only pharmacy in the past five years. A proposal before the working group would allow a technician-staffed dispensing point supervised by a licensed pharmacist through video, with remote prescription verification and counselling. Regulators worry about dispensing errors, counselling quality and who is accountable. Supporters point to pharmacist shortages in remote areas and to the success of hospital telepharmacy during the pandemic. The working group has asked for a briefing on what the international evidence says about the safety and quality of telepharmacy, and what a Jordanian pilot would need in place.

Decision question: Should Jordan permit supervised telepharmacy dispensing points in villages without a pharmacist, and with what safeguards?

Your answer must address these questions:

  1. What models of telepharmacy do the reviews describe, and which are relevant to a village dispensing point?
  2. Appraise the three reviews with AMSTAR 2. Note what kind of studies they include and what that means for confidence.
  3. What did the reviews find about medication safety, adherence, clinical outcomes and patient satisfaction?
  4. Where is the evidence thin or absent for the Jordanian question?
  5. Write the safeguards a pilot would need: regulation, technology, training, documentation and evaluation.

Step 2. Appraise each review with AMSTAR 2

How to rate and how the overall confidence is decided
  • A critical flaw is a No on a critical item (2, 4, 7, 9, 11, 13 or 15). A Partial Yes on a critical item, or a No or Partial Yes on any other item, is a non-critical weakness. “No meta-analysis conducted” is not a flaw.
  • High confidence: no critical flaws and at most one non-critical weakness.
  • Moderate confidence: no critical flaws and more than one non-critical weakness.
  • Low confidence: one critical flaw, with or without non-critical weaknesses.
  • Critically low confidence: more than one critical flaw.

The computed rating below each table applies these rules to your answers. If you disagree, choose your own final rating and say why.
